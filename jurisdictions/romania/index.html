Market research in Romania is the commercial service function through which organisations collect, analyse and interpret evidence about customers, buyers, users, competitors, categories and market opportunities. Assignments may use quantitative surveys, qualitative interviews, focus groups, observation, secondary research, customer data, digital data, experimental testing or other mixed-method approaches.

Romania does not operate a statutory licensing regime specific to commercial market research providers. The service is ordinarily supplied under a commercial research agreement, while its practical framework depends on the business decision, target population, recruitment route, method, processing of identifiable participant information and whether the activity remains genuine research rather than direct marketing.

The principal legal framework combines the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Law No. 190/2018 implementing national measures for the GDPR, Law No. 506/2004 on personal-data processing and privacy in electronic communications, consumer and unfair-commercial-practice rules, and contractual arrangements for confidentiality and intellectual property. The National Supervisory Authority for Personal Data Processing supervises data protection. Research using personal data requires a documented purpose, appropriate legal basis, transparent participant information and safeguards proportionate to the method and risks.

For international businesses, Romania should be designed as a country-specific Central and South-Eastern European market rather than a generic CEE extension. Romanian is normally required for broad consumer and domestic B2B work; Bucharest is economically important but does not represent the country as a whole. Regional differences, urban-rural coverage, target-audience incidence, the Romanian leu, online access, data roles and cross-border transfer arrangements should be resolved before fieldwork. The output is decision-support evidence, not regulatory clearance or a guarantee of market performance.

Core Outcome

A documented evidence base, analysis and set of findings that help the commissioning organisation make a specified commercial, product, customer or market decision.

Object Definition

Market research in Romania is the professional service function concerned with designing and conducting systematic enquiries into Romanian markets, customers, users, buyers, competitors and commercial opportunities. It translates a business question into a research design, gathers primary or secondary evidence, evaluates the quality and limits of that evidence, and communicates findings for use by the commissioning organisation.

DefinitionThe commercial research service used to generate structured evidence about Romanian markets, customers, buyers, users, competitors, products and business opportunities.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commissioned commercial market research for organisations evaluating Romania, Romanian participants or Romania-specific business decisions. It addresses the service line from business briefing through methodology, sample or recruitment design, fieldwork, participant-data governance, analysis, reporting and application of findings.

Covered MattersMarket sizing, market-entry research, customer and buyer research, brand and communications research, proposition and product testing, pricing studies, segmentation, customer-experience research, competitor and channel analysis, B2B interviewing, qualitative and quantitative fieldwork, desk research and insight reporting.
Functional BoundaryThe object explains market research as an evidence and advisory service. It does not provide legal clearance, advertising approval, statistical certification, product-market success or a binding commercial forecast.
Related but Not PrimaryManagement consulting, user-experience research, public-opinion polling, data analytics, marketing strategy, advertising testing, customer-data platforms, academic research and commercial due diligence may overlap but retain different objectives and professional routes.
Outside ScopeActivities whose principal purpose is selling, promoting, fundraising, building direct-marketing lists or covertly influencing participants rather than generating research evidence.

Purpose

The purpose of market research is to reduce uncertainty around an identified commercial decision. A properly scoped Romanian assignment makes clear what the client needs to learn, which evidence can answer the question, which population or market needs to be represented, and what methodological limits must remain visible when findings are used.

PurposeTo generate credible and decision-relevant evidence about a market, audience, proposition, product, price, channel, brand, competitor set or customer experience.
Business ValueResearch can test internal assumptions, identify unmet needs, quantify demand signals, compare alternatives, reveal barriers to adoption and support prioritisation before resources are committed.
Evidence BoundaryFindings are bounded by the selected method, sample, fieldwork period, data quality and analytical assumptions; they should not be presented as certainty beyond the available evidence.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Romanian market research assignment is an evidence-based deliverable that addresses the original business question. Depending on the mandate, this may be a research report, market model, dataset, dashboard, segmentation, tested proposition, customer journey, competitor map or executive presentation that sets out findings, limitations and implications.

Primary OutcomeA documented set of findings and interpretations that answers agreed research questions and supports a defined commercial decision.
Supporting OutputsQuestionnaires, discussion guides, sample records, interview notes, coded data, weighting specifications, transcripts, analytical models, charts, appendices and methodology statements, as applicable.
Decision BoundaryThe research provider produces and explains evidence; the client remains responsible for the commercial decision and for implementation of the findings.

Request Contexts

Market research is normally commissioned when internal, public or syndicated evidence does not resolve a material business question. The request should identify the decision, knowledge gap, intended users, Romania's role in the market definition and the point at which findings are needed.

Request ContextRomanian market entry, product launch, proposition development, pricing review, customer segmentation, brand tracking, acquisition due diligence, channel strategy, customer-experience improvement, competitor assessment or validation of an investment thesis.
Trigger QuestionWhat decision will be made differently if the research produces a reliable answer?
Initial InputsBusiness objectives, existing evidence, target population, geography, category definition, stakeholder assumptions, timing, budget, legal constraints and preferred deliverables.

Typical Users

Market research in Romania is used by domestic and international organisations that need a structured external view of customers or commercial conditions. The commissioning function may sit in strategy, marketing, product, sales, customer experience, investment, innovation, corporate development or Romania country management.

Typical UserRomanian and international companies, scale-ups, investors, private equity firms, professional-services firms, trade bodies, consumer brands, B2B suppliers, technology companies, retailers, financial-services firms, manufacturers, energy and infrastructure businesses and organisations entering or expanding in Romania.
Internal StakeholdersBoards, executive teams, strategy leaders, marketing and brand teams, product managers, customer-experience teams, sales leaders, corporate development teams and country or regional managers.
Procurement ContextAssignments may be procured directly from a specialist agency, through an approved supplier framework, as a workstream within a broader consultancy mandate, or through a multi-country lead agency with Romanian fieldwork support.

Typical Scenarios

Romanian market research assignments range from focused qualitative enquiries to recurring quantitative programmes and integrated market-entry or due-diligence work. The selected method should follow the commercial decision and evidence requirement rather than a provider's preferred tool or an assumption that a regional CEE design represents Romania without local adaptation.

Market EntryAn international company evaluates category conditions, buyer behaviour, routes to market, competitor positions, Romanian-language requirements and barriers before entering Romania.

B2B and Specialist MarketsA provider recruits decision-makers, professionals, channel partners or technical users in technology, manufacturing, retail, financial services, energy, logistics, construction, healthcare or business services, where target populations can be specialised and role verification is material.

Country Characteristics

Romania is a sizeable EU market in Central and South-Eastern Europe, with a major Bucharest-led metropolitan economy, strong technology and services growth, manufacturing and industrial centres, and material urban-rural as well as regional differences. Research design should establish whether the decision concerns Romania nationally, Bucharest, a development region, a named city or a specific industry cluster before defining sample, recruitment and reporting.

Market ContextRomania supports national consumer research and specialised B2B work, but purchasing power, infrastructure, retail, digital access, population density, industry and consumer conditions can vary meaningfully between Bucharest-Ilfov and other development regions.
LanguageRomanian is normally essential for broad consumer research and much domestic B2B work. English can be suitable for defined international, technical or executive audiences, but should not be assumed to substitute for Romanian-language questionnaires, moderation or participant information.
Regional StructureRomania's eight development regions and major cities can be material to commercial design. Bucharest-Ilfov is a distinct metropolitan market, while Transylvania, Banat, Moldavia, Wallachia, Dobruja and other regional contexts can affect sample coverage, category use and interpretation.
Data EnvironmentThe National Institute of Statistics (INSSE) is the main producer and coordinator of Romania's official statistics. Its TEMPO-Online database, business-demography publications, regional and county directorates and statistical business register provide a significant secondary evidence base for market framing and analysis.
Digital ResearchOnline surveys, panels, remote interviews, digital communities and behavioural methods are operationally accessible, but online coverage, device access, cookies, data minimisation, participant information and respondent-quality controls should be designed into the methodology.
CEE AssumptionRomanian findings should not automatically be treated as representative of Poland, Hungary, Bulgaria, the Czech Republic or another CEE market. Language, purchasing power, regional structure, retail, media, infrastructure and consumer behaviour differ.

Process Flow

There is no universal statutory process for Romanian commercial market research. A defensible professional assignment normally moves from the business decision and research brief through methodology, sample or recruitment design, instrument development, participant communication, fieldwork, quality control, analysis, reporting and controlled data closure.

1. Define the DecisionSpecify the commercial decision, learning objectives, hypotheses, stakeholders, Romanian market boundary, regional scope and the actions the research is intended to inform.
2. Audit Existing EvidenceReview client data, previous research, INSSE and other official statistics, market sources and known information gaps before commissioning new fieldwork.
3. Design the MethodSelect qualitative, quantitative, observational, behavioural, secondary or mixed methods; define the sample, recruitment route, analytical approach and limitations.
4. Establish Data GovernanceAllocate controller and processor roles, identify lawful bases, prepare participant information, set security and retention controls, and assess transfers, cookies, tracking, profiling or direct-contact requirements.
5. Develop and Test InstrumentsPrepare the questionnaire, discussion guide, stimulus material, Romanian translation and coding framework; pilot or soft-launch where proportionate.
6. Recruit and Conduct FieldworkRecruit eligible participants, verify relevant characteristics, collect responses or conduct interviews, manage incentives and monitor fieldwork quality.
7. Process and Validate DataClean, code, transcribe, weight or structure the material; document exclusions, quality checks, sample performance and material deviations.
8. Analyse and InterpretAnswer the research questions, distinguish evidence from inference, test competing explanations and identify methodological, regional and segment limitations.
9. Deliver and ClosePresent findings in the agreed format, transfer authorised files, explain methods and limitations, and archive, delete or anonymise material under agreed retention and data-governance arrangements.

Decision Tree

The research route should be determined by the business decision, existing evidence, target population and required level of confidence. Compliance review should occur before fieldwork where the project involves identifiable people, special-category data, cookies or similar technologies, direct electronic contact, recordings, profiling, regional or urban-rural population differences, or an unclear boundary between neutral research and direct marketing.

Is the question already answerable from reliable internal or official data?If yes, begin with secondary analysis and define the residual evidence gap before commissioning primary research.
Does the decision concern Romania nationally, Bucharest, a named development region or a local market?If it is territorial, design quotas, recruitment, fieldwork and reporting accordingly rather than treating Bucharest or a generic CEE sample as a proxy for the country.
Is the objective exploratory or explanatory?If yes, qualitative interviews, groups, observation or desk research may be appropriate before measurement.
Is the objective to estimate incidence, compare groups or track change?If yes, use a quantitative design with an explicit target population, sample source, questionnaire, weighting plan and uncertainty limitations.
Will the project process identifiable personal data?If yes, document roles, purpose, lawful basis, transparency, minimisation, security, retention, deletion or anonymisation, participant rights and transfer safeguards before collection.
Will cookies, device identifiers or passive tracking be used?If yes, assess Law No. 506/2004 and GDPR requirements before deployment, including the participant information and consent framework applicable to terminal-device access.
Will invitations or follow-up communications contain selling, promotion or lead generation?If yes, separate that activity from research and assess consumer, direct-marketing and unfair-commercial-practice rules; it should not be represented to participants as neutral research.
Will a global provider or technology stack process Romanian participant data?If yes, map controller and processor roles, subprocessors, international transfers, security measures, tracking technologies, retention and deletion arrangements before collection.
Decision logic: Define the commercial decision and Romanian territorial scope first. Select the method and sample only after the target population and confidence need are clear, and resolve language, data-governance, cookie, direct-contact and research-marketing questions before recruitment or data collection begins.

Cross-Border Relevance

Romanian market research is frequently commissioned within Central and South-Eastern European, EU or global programmes. International consistency can be valuable, but it should not override Romanian language, regional structure, country-specific market conditions, GDPR roles, electronic communications rules, direct-marketing boundaries or the need to report Romanian findings separately from a regional aggregate.

RecognitionMarket research is a commercial professional service rather than a licensed Romanian professional title. Supplier selection therefore turns on methodological competence, quality controls, sector knowledge, data governance and contractual accountability.
Foreign CompaniesForeign clients and research providers may commission or conduct Romanian research, but participant contact, personal-data processing, cookies, subcontracting, data transfers and research-marketing classification should comply with Romanian and applicable EU requirements.
Language ConsiderationRomanian is normally appropriate for broad consumer research and much domestic B2B work. English can be suitable for defined international, specialist or executive audiences, but language choice and translations should be validated against the actual target group and decision context.
International RulesGDPR governs personal-data processing across the EU and EEA. Transfers outside the EEA require a valid transfer mechanism and appropriate assessment, while Romanian electronic communications and consumer rules can apply to direct contact and online technologies.
Practical ConsiderationMulti-country designs should harmonise core measures while retaining country-specific samples, translations, category definitions, Romanian leu price points, stimuli, official sources and interpretation. Romania's regional and urban-rural structure can require separate quotas or analysis.
Typical RiskAssuming Romania can be represented by a generic CEE sample, importing a questionnaire without Romanian review, using Bucharest as a national proxy, or using global recruitment and technology vendors without mapping data roles, cookies and transfer safeguards.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The central commercial risk is not merely inaccurate data; it is evidence that appears precise but does not answer the client's actual decision. Methodological weakness, unsuitable recruitment, poor Romanian localisation, uncontrolled data sources, excessive inference, unclear data responsibilities and blurred research-marketing boundaries can undermine both usefulness and compliance.

Briefing RiskAn activity-led brief can produce interviews or survey data without resolving the commercial decision the client needs to make.
Regional RiskA nationally labelled study that is concentrated in Bucharest or a limited set of major cities can misstate differences in income, infrastructure, industry, retail, digital access and consumer behaviour across Romanian regions.
Coverage RiskA sample frame, online panel or recruitment source may exclude parts of the target population or over-represent frequent research participants.
Low-Incidence RiskSpecialist B2B buyers, professionals or rare consumer segments may be difficult to identify and verify, increasing time, cost and recruitment error.
Measurement RiskLeading wording, poor Romanian translation, order effects, unrealistic stimuli or inconsistent interviewing can distort the evidence.
Quality and Fraud RiskDuplicate, inattentive, automated or misrepresented respondents can affect online samples unless validation and detection controls are proportionate and documented.
Data Protection RiskCollecting excessive identifiers, recording without adequate information, retaining material beyond necessity, or sharing identifiable respondent data without a valid framework can create GDPR and Romanian-law exposure.
Cookie and Contact RiskUsing cookies, device identifiers or direct electronic contact without a documented legal analysis, suitable participant information and required consent mechanisms can create electronic-communications and privacy exposure.
Direct-Marketing RiskUsing survey invitations, answers or participant profiles for sales, promotion or lead generation without a separately compliant basis can change the purpose of processing and create consumer, privacy and communications-law exposure.
AI and Automation RiskAutomated transcription, coding, translation or synthesis may introduce errors, disclose confidential data or obscure the evidence trail unless tools, human review and data handling are controlled.

FAQ

Is market research a regulated profession in Romania?No dedicated Romanian licensing regime applies to market research providers as a profession. Specific activities remain subject to GDPR, Romanian personal-data and electronic communications law, consumer and unfair-commercial-practice law, contract, confidentiality, intellectual property and sector-specific rules where applicable.
Does GDPR apply to surveys and interviews?Yes, where collected information relates to an identified or identifiable person. Truly anonymised information is outside GDPR, but coded or pseudonymised data remain personal data if a person can be re-identified.
Can public Romanian business statistics be used for B2B research?Yes. INSSE official statistics, business demography and data resources can support market framing and business research. The provider should still assess the source conditions, personal-data implications of any individual contact information and whether intended outreach is genuine research or direct marketing.
Are cookies permitted for online research in Romania?Cookies and comparable terminal-device access require analysis under Law No. 506/2004 and GDPR. The provider should document the purpose, participant information and consent route applicable to the specific technology and deployment.
Can survey participants be sent marketing after the study?Not automatically. Research participation and marketing are separate purposes. Any reuse needs a distinct lawful-basis and transparency assessment, and electronic communications or consumer rules may also apply.
Is participant consent always the GDPR lawful basis?No. The appropriate lawful basis depends on the processing operation and the parties' roles. Consent may be suitable in some cases, while other GDPR bases can apply; special-category data require an additional condition.
Must Romanian consumer research be conducted in Romanian?There is no universal rule requiring every commercial study to use Romanian, but language must be understandable and appropriate for the target population. Romanian is normally the safer default for broad consumer and domestic B2B research.
Can Romania be combined into one CEE or European sample?Yes, where the business decision supports a regional design, but country composition, weighting, language, territory and reporting must be explicit. A CEE or European aggregate should not be presented as a Romania estimate without an adequate Romanian base.
