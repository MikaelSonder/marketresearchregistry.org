Market research in the United States is the commercial service function through which organisations collect, analyse and interpret evidence about consumers, business buyers, competitors, categories, channels and commercial opportunities. Engagements can combine quantitative surveys, qualitative interviews, focus groups, ethnography, online communities, customer data, desk research, observation, behavioural sources and mixed-method programmes.

The United States does not have a single nationwide professional licensing regime for commercial market research providers or a single omnibus federal consumer privacy law. The legal framework is functional and layered: federal consumer-protection, sectoral privacy, communications, intellectual-property and competition rules interact with state privacy, data-broker, biometric, wiretapping, recording and consumer laws. The relevant law depends on where participants reside, where the provider and client operate, data type, communication method, sector and intended use of findings.

The Federal Trade Commission is a key federal consumer-protection and privacy enforcement body. The US market research profession is also shaped by the Insights Association Code of Standards and Ethics, which emphasises voluntary participation, transparency, consent, participant protection, a clear separation between research and non-research activity, and restrictions on using research-derived data to identify or act directly toward an individual without consent.

The United States is a large federal market with major state, regional, demographic, cultural, language, income and channel variation. A result from California, New York, a metropolitan area, a panel, a customer list, an online platform or an English-only sample should not be presented as US-national evidence unless target definition, sampling, weighting and stated limitations support that claim.

Core Outcome

A documented evidence base, analysis and set of findings that supports a defined market, product, customer, brand, investment or commercial decision.

Object Definition

Market research in the United States is the professional service of converting a business question into a structured research design, collecting or organising relevant evidence, evaluating that evidence and communicating findings, limitations and implications to the commissioning organisation.

DefinitionThe commercial research service used to generate structured evidence about US markets, consumers, business buyers, users, competitors, products, channels and commercial opportunities.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commissioned market research as a commercial service line for organisations evaluating the United States or US-based respondent populations. It covers work from business briefing and evidence review through methodology, recruitment, fieldwork, analysis, reporting and practical commercial application.

Covered MattersMarket sizing, consumer and buyer research, brand and communications research, product and concept testing, pricing studies, segmentation, customer-experience research, competitor and channel analysis, B2B interviewing, qualitative and quantitative fieldwork, desk research and insight reporting.
Functional BoundaryThe object describes market research as an evidence and advisory service. It does not itself provide legal clearance, official statistical approval, product registration, advertising approval or a guarantee of market performance.
Related but Not PrimaryManagement consulting, user-experience research, public-opinion polling, social research, data analytics, competitive intelligence, marketing strategy, advertising testing, customer-data platforms, academic research and commercial due diligence may overlap but retain separate objectives.
Outside ScopeActivities whose principal purpose is direct selling, promotion, fundraising, list building or covert influence rather than generation of research evidence.

Purpose

The purpose of market research is to reduce uncertainty around a defined commercial decision. A well-scoped US assignment identifies what the client needs to learn, which evidence can answer the question, how the relevant market or population should be represented and what limitations remain after the work is complete.

PurposeTo generate credible and decision-relevant evidence about a US market, audience, proposition, product, price, channel, brand, competitor set or customer experience.
Business ValueResearch can challenge assumptions, identify unmet needs, quantify demand signals, compare alternatives, reveal entry barriers and support prioritisation before capital or management resources are committed.
Evidence BoundaryFindings are bounded by method, sample, recruitment source, fieldwork period, response quality, state coverage, analytical assumptions and population definition; they should not be presented as certainty beyond that evidence.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a US market research assignment is an evidence-based deliverable aligned with the commissioning decision. Depending on scope, this may be a market report, dataset, dashboard, segmentation, customer journey, tested proposition, demand model, competitor map or executive presentation.

Primary OutcomeA documented set of findings and interpretations that answers agreed research questions and supports a defined commercial decision.
Supporting OutputsQuestionnaires, discussion guides, sample records, interview notes, coded data, weighting specifications, recordings, transcripts, analytical models, charts, appendices and methodology statements where included in scope.
Decision BoundaryThe research provider produces and explains evidence; the client retains responsibility for commercial decisions and implementation.
Regulatory BoundaryA favourable research result is not product approval, advertising clearance, a licence or official endorsement in the United States.

Request Contexts

Market research is normally commissioned when a business decision cannot be supported adequately by internal information alone. The request should identify the decision, knowledge gap, users of the findings, US market boundary and point at which evidence is required.

Request ContextUS market entry, product launch, pricing review, customer segmentation, brand tracking, acquisition due diligence, state expansion, customer-experience improvement, competitor assessment or validation of an investment thesis.
Trigger QuestionWhat decision will be made differently if the research produces a reliable answer?

Country Characteristics

The United States is a large federal market with substantial variation among states, regions, cities, metropolitan and rural areas, demographics, languages, income groups, industries, media ecosystems, digital platforms and regulatory environments. Research design should distinguish US-national, regional, state, local and segment-level claims rather than treat the country as a homogeneous market.

Operational CultureUS assignments commonly require a clear commercial brief, defined decision users, transparent methodology, rapid project communication, defensible quality controls and explicit allocation of client, agency, panel, platform and data-vendor roles.
LanguageEnglish is primary for many studies, but Spanish and other languages may be essential for specific populations. Language selection should follow the target population, literacy, channel, culture and research objective rather than a national default.
Digital EnvironmentOnline panels, mobile surveys, digital communities, remote interviews, social listening, e-commerce data and behavioural methods are widely used. State privacy law, platform terms, online identifiers, cookies, tracking and session recording may be relevant.
Market StructureThe US combines large domestic and multinational businesses, federal and state regulation, major technology and retail platforms, diverse media markets, mature consumer categories and substantial regional variation.
Geographic CoverageCalifornia, New York, Texas, Florida, Illinois and other major markets are commercially significant, but each is not automatically representative of US-national demand or regulatory exposure.
Population RepresentationRace, ethnicity, language, age, income, disability, education, employment, geography, household type, immigration experience and digital access can all be material variables. Sampling and reporting should state intended coverage and limitations.
Official Data EnvironmentThe US Census Bureau provides demographic, economic, housing, income, business and community data through national data tools, tables, maps and other official resources.
Professional EnvironmentThe Insights Association Code of Standards and Ethics provides a self-regulatory professional framework for market research and data analytics, including duty of care, transparency, consent, data protection and clear research/non-research separation.

Process Flow

There is no universal statutory sequence for a US commercial market research assignment. A professionally structured mandate commonly moves from the business decision and evidence audit through methodology, federal and state legal mapping, recruitment, fieldwork, quality control, analysis and decision-oriented delivery.

1. Define the DecisionSpecify commercial decision, research objectives, hypotheses, stakeholders, US geographic scope and intended actions.
2. Audit Existing EvidenceReview client information, earlier research, Census Bureau and other federal or state data, sector sources and evidence gaps before commissioning primary fieldwork.
3. Design the MethodSelect qualitative, quantitative, observational, behavioural, secondary or mixed methods; define the target population, sample, recruitment route, analysis and limitations.
4. Map Legal and Data ControlsIdentify participant states, federal and state laws, personal data, sensitive data, tracking, recordings, communications, sector context, vendors, retention, sharing and international access.
5. Localise and Test InstrumentsPrepare US English, Spanish and other target-language instruments as appropriate; test terminology, scales, accessibility, examples, routing and informed participant communication.
6. Recruit and Conduct FieldworkRecruit eligible participants, verify relevant characteristics, collect responses or conduct interviews, manage incentives and monitor quotas and field quality.
7. Process and Validate DataClean, code, transcribe, translate, weight or structure material; document exclusions, fraud controls, quality checks, sample performance and deviations.
8. Analyse and InterpretAnswer research questions, distinguish evidence from inference, test competing explanations and identify state, regional, demographic, methodological and analytical limitations.
9. Deliver and ApplyPresent findings in the agreed format, transfer authorised files, explain methodology and limitations, and support stakeholder decision-making.

Decision Tree

The research route should be determined by the decision, available evidence, target population and required confidence. Federal, state, sector and communications review should occur before recruitment where the project uses personal data, sensitive data, children’s information, health information, recordings, biometrics, tracking, customer lists, global platforms or contact methods such as SMS and telephone.

Is the question answerable from reliable internal, Census Bureau or other secondary data?If yes, begin with secondary analysis and define the remaining evidence gap before commissioning primary research.
Is the objective exploratory or explanatory?If yes, qualitative interviews, focus groups, ethnography, observation, diary studies or desk research may be appropriate before measurement.
Is the objective to estimate incidence, compare groups or track change?If yes, use a quantitative design with an explicit population, sample source, questionnaire, weighting approach and stated limitations.
Which states do participants reside in?Map participant residence and relevant state privacy, consumer, biometric, recording, data-broker, health and communications laws before collection or vendor deployment.
Will personal, sensitive, biometric, health or children’s data be collected?If yes, define legal basis, notices, consent where required, minimisation, security, vendor roles, rights handling and age or parental-consent controls before collection.
Will telephone, SMS, email or other electronic outreach be used?If yes, assess TCPA, CAN-SPAM, state telemarketing, state mini-TCPA, do-not-call and communications rules based on the message, recipient, technology and content.
Will interviews, focus groups, user tests or sessions be recorded or tracked?If yes, assess recording consent, wiretap, biometric, session-replay, web analytics, platform and state-law conditions before enabling recording or tracking.
Does the project involve healthcare, finance, education, children, employment, government data or another sector-specific context?If yes, obtain specialist federal, state, contractual and sector review before activity begins.
Will participant data be accessed outside the United States?If yes, map recipient entities, countries, systems, contractual controls, state requirements and security safeguards before international access is enabled.
Decision logic: Define the decision first, then identify the minimum evidence capable of answering it. Select the method only after the population and confidence requirement are clear, and complete federal, state, sector, communications and recording analysis before deployment.

Cross-Border Relevance

US market research is frequently commissioned by global organisations and delivered through US agencies, international research networks, panels, cloud services, transcription, translation and analytics platforms. International consistency can be valuable, but it must not erase US state-law, sector, communications, recording, sampling and participant-protection distinctions.

RecognitionMarket research is a commercial professional service rather than a universally licensed US professional title. Supplier evaluation focuses on methodology, US category experience, federal and state compliance capability, participant protection, fieldwork quality and contractual accountability.
Foreign CompaniesForeign clients and agencies may commission US research, but US participant data, personal information, state privacy, communications, recording, vendors, sector rules and international access should be assessed under applicable federal and state laws.
Language ConsiderationEnglish is widely relevant but Spanish and other language materials may be needed for defined audiences. The language should follow the target population and methodology rather than a national default.
International RulesThere is no single US data-export law for all commercial research data, but federal sector laws, state privacy laws, contractual commitments, foreign privacy law, security requirements and participant notices can govern cross-border data access.
Data HostingBefore using a global survey, panel, recording, transcription, analytics or AI platform, identify data locations, recipient entities, remote access, state residency, data categories, security, contracts and applicable notice or consent controls.
Regional AssumptionUS findings should not automatically be generalised to Canada, Latin America, Europe, Australia or an English-speaking market. Population, regulation, market size, culture, channels and privacy law differ.
Typical RiskCollecting multi-state respondent data and applying a single generic privacy notice, or enabling recordings, SMS recruitment, tracking or global access without a state-specific and sector-specific assessment.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The principal commercial risk is not simply collecting inaccurate data; it is producing evidence that appears precise but does not answer the client’s decision, does not represent the claimed US population or is undermined by preventable federal, state, sector or participant-protection weaknesses.

Briefing RiskAn activity-led brief can produce interviews or survey data without resolving the actual commercial decision.
State-Law RiskAssuming a single US privacy position can be inaccurate because participant residence, business threshold, data category, exemption, communications technology and sector can trigger different state requirements.
Coverage RiskAn online panel, customer list, metropolitan source or platform audience may exclude or over-represent relevant parts of the target population.
National-Claim RiskEvidence from one state, region, city, demographic group or digital cohort may be overstated as US-national evidence.
Language and Accessibility RiskEnglish-only materials, inaccessible survey design or unsuitable literacy assumptions can exclude relevant audiences and distort results.
Measurement RiskLeading wording, inappropriate scales, interviewer effects, unrealistic stimuli, panel conditioning or device-unfriendly surveys can distort evidence.
Quality and Fraud RiskDuplicate, inattentive, automated or misrepresented respondents can affect digital samples unless detection and validation controls are proportionate and documented.
Privacy RiskCollecting unnecessary identifiers, using inadequate disclosures, retaining data indefinitely, ignoring sensitive-data rights or failing to control vendors can create federal, state and contractual exposure.
Communications RiskTelephone, SMS, email or other outreach can trigger TCPA, CAN-SPAM, state telemarketing, do-not-call or state mini-TCPA requirements depending on the method and content.
Recording and Biometric RiskAudio, video, screen recording, session replay, facial analysis, voice analysis or biometric collection can raise consent, wiretap, biometric, privacy and security obligations.
Research-Marketing Boundary RiskCombining research contact with sales, promotion or lead generation can mislead participants and trigger additional legal and ethical obligations. The IA Code requires a clear distinction between research and non-research activity.
Interpretation RiskSmall qualitative samples, opt-in panels, modelled estimates and subgroup results may be generalised beyond what the method supports.
Confidentiality RiskUnreleased products, investment theses, client identities or identifiable interviews may be exposed through weak agency, facility, platform or reporting controls.
AI and Automation RiskAutomated transcription, coding, synthetic moderation, AI avatars or synthesis may introduce error, disclose confidential data, create biometric or tracking issues or obscure the evidence trail unless systems and controls are documented.

FAQ

The following questions address common commercial and operational issues for organisations commissioning market research in the United States.

Is market research a regulated profession in the United States?No dedicated national professional licence generally applies to commercial market research providers. The relevant requirements arise from federal, state and sector laws, contracts and professional standards.
Is there one US privacy law for market research?No. The United States has federal consumer-protection and sector laws plus state privacy frameworks. The applicable position depends on the participants, organisation, data, state residency, technology, sector and context.
Do state privacy laws apply to research participants?They can. Many state laws have thresholds, exemptions and research-relevant definitions. Map participant states, data categories, organisation role and current statutory requirements before relying on a nationwide approach.
Can US research participants be contacted by text message or telephone?Potentially, but the method requires review under TCPA, FCC rules, state telemarketing, mini-TCPA, do-not-call and consent requirements. The technology and message content matter as much as whether the activity is labelled research.
Can sessions be recorded?Potentially, but recording may trigger state all-party or one-party consent rules, biometric considerations, privacy notices, platform terms and client-security requirements. Obtain a state-specific recording analysis before recording.
Can participant data be hosted outside the United States?Potentially, but federal sector laws, state privacy rules, contracts, client policies, participant notices and security safeguards must be evaluated for the data flow. There is no single answer for all US research.
Can a client receive raw respondent data?Only where delivery is compatible with purpose, privacy notices, consent where required, state law, confidentiality, minimisation and contract. Aggregated or de-identified delivery is often preferable.
Can a California or New York sample represent the United States?Not automatically. Any national claim should correspond to the sampling frame, including states, regions, demographics, language, income, urban-rural coverage, online access and other relevant dimensions.
Can research recruitment include a sales offer?Research and commercial solicitation should be separated. Mixed activity can trigger participant trust, FTC, state consumer law, TCPA, CAN-SPAM and professional-ethics issues.
Can research findings be used in advertising?Yes, but public claims should be accurate, supportable and not misleading. The research design, population, question wording, analysis and limitations should support the message actually communicated.
