Market research in Quebec is the commercial service function through which organisations collect, analyse and interpret evidence about Quebec consumers, business buyers, competitors, categories, channels and commercial opportunities. Engagements can combine quantitative surveys, qualitative interviews, focus groups, ethnography, online communities, customer data, desk research, observation, behavioural sources and mixed-method programmes.

Quebec does not generally require a dedicated professional licence for commercial market research providers. The service is normally commissioned under a commercial engagement and is shaped by the decision to be supported, the Quebec target population, sampling and recruitment, methodology, French-language requirements, privacy and data-governance position, analytical standard and intended use of findings.

Quebec has a distinctive private-sector privacy framework. The Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, substantially modernised by Law 25, applies broadly to private enterprises that collect, hold, use or communicate personal information in Quebec. It requires organisations to designate a person in charge of personal information protection, maintain governance policies, apply privacy by default in qualifying technology settings, conduct privacy impact assessments for projects involving personal information and before communicating personal information outside Quebec, and manage confidentiality incidents. The Commission d’accès à l’information (CAI) is the provincial privacy regulator.

French is the official language of Quebec. Market research must assess whether French-language participant materials, questionnaires, discussion guides, recruitment, privacy notices, consent wording, reports and customer communications are required or appropriate. Quebec findings should not automatically be treated as Canadian findings, and Montreal results should not automatically represent Quebec-wide consumers, regions, French-speaking populations or all provincial markets.

Object Definition

Market research in Quebec is the professional service of converting a business question into a structured research design, collecting or organising relevant evidence, evaluating that evidence and communicating findings, limitations and implications to the commissioning organisation.

Scope

The Registry Object covers commissioned market research as a commercial service line for organisations evaluating Quebec or Quebec-based respondent populations. It covers work from business briefing and evidence review through methodology, recruitment, French-language localisation, fieldwork, analysis, reporting and practical commercial application.

Covered MattersMarket sizing, consumer and buyer research, brand and communications research, product and concept testing, pricing studies, segmentation, customer-experience research, competitor and channel analysis, B2B interviewing, qualitative and quantitative fieldwork, desk research and insight reporting.
Functional BoundaryThe object describes market research as an evidence and advisory service. It does not itself provide legal clearance, official statistical approval, product registration, advertising approval or a guarantee of market performance.
Related but Not PrimaryManagement consulting, user-experience research, public-opinion polling, social research, data analytics, competitive intelligence, marketing strategy, advertising testing, customer-data platforms, academic research and commercial due diligence may overlap but retain separate objectives.
Outside ScopeActivities whose principal purpose is direct selling, promotion, fundraising, list building or covert influence rather than generation of research evidence.

Purpose

The purpose of market research is to reduce uncertainty around a specified commercial decision. A well-scoped Quebec assignment identifies what the client needs to learn, which evidence can answer the question, how the relevant population should be represented and what limitations remain after the work is complete.

PurposeTo generate credible and decision-relevant evidence about a Quebec market, audience, proposition, product, price, channel, brand, competitor set or customer experience.
Business ValueResearch can challenge assumptions, identify unmet needs, quantify demand signals, localise global propositions, compare alternatives, reveal entry barriers and support prioritisation before capital or management resources are committed.
Evidence BoundaryFindings are bounded by method, sample, recruitment source, fieldwork period, response quality, French-language and regional coverage, analytical assumptions and target population; they should not be represented as certainty beyond that evidence.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Quebec market research assignment is an evidence-based deliverable aligned with the commissioning decision. It may be a market report, dataset, dashboard, segmentation, customer journey, tested proposition, demand model, competitor map or executive presentation.

Primary OutcomeA documented set of findings and interpretations that answers agreed research questions and supports a defined commercial decision.
Supporting OutputsQuestionnaires, discussion guides, sample records, interview notes, coded data, weighting specifications, recordings, transcripts, analytical models, charts, appendices and methodology statements where included in scope.
Decision BoundaryThe research provider produces and explains evidence; the client retains responsibility for commercial decisions and implementation.
Regulatory BoundaryA favourable research result is not product approval, advertising clearance, a licence or official endorsement in Quebec or Canada.

Country Characteristics

Quebec is a distinct Canadian province with French as its official language, a separate civil-law tradition, a modernised private-sector privacy regime and significant regional, cultural, demographic and commercial variation. Research design should distinguish Quebec-wide, Montreal, Quebec City, regional, rural, French-speaking, English-speaking, Indigenous and segment-level claims rather than treat Quebec as a simple extension of English Canada.

Operational CultureQuebec assignments commonly require clear commercial scope, French-language capability, privacy by design, Law 25 governance, accessible participant communication, transparent methodology and explicit allocation of client, agency, panel, platform and data-vendor roles.
LanguageFrench is Quebec’s official language. French-language materials are central for broad consumer research and many commercial communications. English and other language materials may be relevant to defined populations, but English-only research can create coverage, accessibility, legal and measurement risk.
Digital EnvironmentOnline panels, mobile surveys, digital communities, remote interviews, user testing, social listening, e-commerce data and behavioural methods are widely used. Law 25 privacy by default, cookies, identifiers, tracking, profiling, automated decision-making, recordings, biometrics, data sharing and vendor controls require project-specific assessment.
Market StructureQuebec includes major aerospace, AI and technology, financial, life sciences, manufacturing, energy, retail, media, culture, food, tourism, public-sector and professional-services ecosystems.
Geographic CoverageMontreal, Laval, Longueuil, Quebec City, Gatineau, Sherbrooke, Trois-Rivières, Saguenay, Estrie, Mauricie, Abitibi-Témiscamingue, Gaspésie, Côte-Nord, Nord-du-Québec and rural regions can have different populations, economies, languages, access, channels and customer behaviour.
Population RepresentationRegion, French or English language preference, age, income, ethnicity, immigration experience, disability, Indigenous identity, urban-rural context, occupation, household type and digital access may be material variables. Sampling and reporting should state intended coverage and limitations.
Official Data EnvironmentInstitut de la statistique du Québec is the public body responsible for reliable, objective and relevant statistical information on Quebec society. It publishes social, economic, demographic, cultural and environmental data for Quebec, administrative regions and regional county municipalities.
Privacy EnvironmentLaw 25 strengthened Quebec’s private and public-sector privacy framework, with extensive requirements relevant to governance, privacy impact assessment, confidentiality incidents, cross-border communication, automated decisions, technology use, biometrics and rights. Quebec privacy analysis should be treated as separate from PIPEDA analysis.

Process Flow

There is no universal statutory sequence for a Quebec commercial market research assignment. A professionally structured mandate commonly moves from business decision and evidence audit through methodology, Law 25 and language review, recruitment, fieldwork, quality control, analysis and decision-oriented delivery.

1. Define the DecisionSpecify commercial decision, research objectives, hypotheses, stakeholders, Quebec geographic and language scope and intended actions.
2. Audit Existing EvidenceReview client information, previous research, ISQ, Statistics Canada, Quebec regional data and sector sources before commissioning primary fieldwork.
3. Design the MethodSelect qualitative, quantitative, observational, behavioural, secondary or mixed methods; define target population, sample, recruitment route, analysis and limitations.
4. Map Law 25 and Data ControlsIdentify private enterprise or public body status, personal information, consent, privacy officer, technology, privacy impact assessment, out-of-Quebec communication, vendors, security, retention, rights and confidentiality-incident processes.
5. Localise and Test InstrumentsPrepare Quebec French, English and other target-language materials as needed; test terminology, translation equivalence, accessibility, cultural context, scales, privacy disclosures and participant communication.
6. Recruit and Conduct FieldworkRecruit eligible participants, verify characteristics, collect responses or conduct interviews, manage incentives and monitor quotas, regional coverage, language preference and field quality.
7. Process and Validate DataClean, code, transcribe, translate, weight or structure material; document exclusions, fraud controls, quality checks, sample performance, privacy controls and deviations.
8. Analyse and InterpretAnswer research questions, distinguish evidence from inference, test competing explanations and identify Quebec regional, language, Indigenous, privacy, category and methodological limitations.
9. Deliver and ApplyPresent findings in agreed French, English or bilingual format, transfer authorised files under suitable safeguards, explain methodology and limitations, and support stakeholder decision-making.

Decision Tree

The research route should be determined by the decision, available evidence, target population and required confidence. Law 25, French language, Indigenous, communications, special data, technology, recording, sector and out-of-Quebec communication review should occur before recruitment where personal data, health data, cookies, customer data, automated processing, global platforms or promotional contact are involved.

Is the question answerable from reliable internal, ISQ, Statistics Canada or other secondary data?If yes, begin with secondary analysis and define the remaining evidence gap before commissioning primary research.
Is the target Quebec-wide, Montreal, regional, rural, French-speaking, English-speaking, Indigenous, customer-only, B2B or otherwise bounded?Define the population before choosing method and sample. Do not turn Montreal, English-only, French-only or a city sample into a Quebec-wide or Canada-wide claim without adequate coverage.
Does the project require French-language participant materials?If yes, design Quebec French questionnaires, privacy notices, consents, recruitment, moderation, translation equivalence, accessibility and reporting arrangements before fieldwork.
Is the objective exploratory or explanatory?If yes, qualitative interviews, focus groups, ethnography, observation, diary studies or desk research may be appropriate before measurement.
Is the objective to estimate incidence, compare groups or track change?If yes, use a quantitative design with explicit population, sample source, questionnaire, language route, weighting approach and stated limitations.
Will a private enterprise collect, hold, use or communicate personal information in Quebec?If yes, assess Law 25 and the Private Sector Act: person in charge, privacy governance, purpose, consent, transparency, minimisation, rights, security, confidentiality incidents, vendors, retention and data communication.
Will an information system, technology, profiling, location, biometric, automated decision or out-of-Quebec data communication be used?If yes, assess the need for a privacy impact assessment, privacy by default, transparent technology notice, biometric filing or notice, automated-decision transparency, transfer safeguards and current CAI guidance before deployment.
Will email, SMS, calls, cookies, pixels, session replay or online identifiers be used?If yes, assess CASL, Quebec privacy law, telecom and contact-method requirements, research-marketing boundary, consent, opt-outs and platform controls before deployment.
Will participant data be shared with a client or accessed outside Quebec or Canada?If yes, map organisation roles, purpose, data categories, sharing terms, privacy impact assessment, out-of-Quebec risk analysis, safeguards, transparency, retention and applicable PIPEDA or foreign law before access begins.
Does the project involve First Nations, Inuit or Métis communities or data?If yes, assess early engagement, community protocol, data sovereignty, governance, permissions, benefit, confidentiality, ethics and reporting arrangements before recruitment.
Does participant contact include sales, promotion, fundraising, political lobbying or lead generation?If yes, separate it from research and assess CASL, Quebec privacy, consumer, advertising and ethical obligations. Do not conduct non-research activity under the guise of research.
Decision logic: Define the decision and Quebec target population first. Select the method only after regional, French-language and confidence requirements are clear, then complete Law 25, privacy impact, technology, communication, sharing and sector analysis before deployment.

Cross-Border Relevance

Quebec market research is frequently commissioned by Canadian, US, European and global organisations and delivered through Quebec agencies, international research networks, panels, cloud services, transcription, translation and analytics platforms. International consistency can be valuable, but it must not remove Quebec Law 25, French-language, provincial-market, privacy-impact, data-sharing and cross-border distinctions.

RecognitionMarket research is a commercial professional service rather than a universally licensed Quebec professional title. Supplier evaluation focuses on methodology, Quebec French capability, Law 25 competence, regional experience, participant protection, fieldwork quality and contractual accountability.
Foreign CompaniesForeign clients and agencies may commission Quebec research, but Quebec personal information, Law 25 applicability, privacy officer, impact assessment, French-language material, technology, biometrics, vendors, sector rules and global data access require explicit legal and contractual analysis.
Language ConsiderationQuebec French is normally expected for broad consumer research and commercial communication. English and other language versions may be relevant but should not displace French where the target population or applicable law requires French-language treatment.
Canada and US BoundaryQuebec has a distinct provincial private-sector privacy regime. Quebec research should not be governed solely by PIPEDA, US privacy law or a global privacy template. Quebec data and out-of-Quebec communications require their own Law 25 assessment.
Data HostingBefore using a global survey, panel, recording, transcription, analytics or AI platform, identify data locations, Quebec private or public sector role, recipient entities, out-of-Quebec communication, privacy impact assessment, security, retention, privacy notice and rights-management controls.
Regional AssumptionQuebec findings should not automatically be generalised to Canada, Ontario, French-speaking Europe, the United States or another French-speaking market. Language, law, culture, market structure, consumer behaviour and channels differ.
Typical RiskUsing Quebec respondent data in a Canadian, US or global vendor chain without completing a Law 25 privacy impact assessment for out-of-Quebec communication, properly mapping parties and safeguards, or providing suitable French-language notices and participant information.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The principal commercial risk is not simply collecting inaccurate data; it is producing evidence that appears precise but does not answer the client’s decision, does not represent the claimed Quebec population or is undermined by Law 25, French-language, privacy-impact, technology, data-sharing or professional-ethics weaknesses.

Briefing RiskAn activity-led brief can produce interviews or survey data without resolving the actual commercial decision.
Quebec-Wide Claim RiskMontreal-centric, English-only, French-only, urban, customer-list or online-panel evidence may be overstated as Quebec-wide evidence without appropriate regional, rural, language, age, income and population coverage.
French Language RiskUsing inadequate Quebec French, English-only materials or non-equivalent translations can exclude participants, alter meaning, reduce response quality and create language-law or commercial communication risk.
Law 25 Governance RiskFailing to designate responsibility, document governance, define purpose, maintain transparency, limit information, control vendors, manage incidents or keep current records can create legal and commercial exposure.
Privacy Impact RiskDeploying technology, communicating personal information outside Quebec, profiling, using location or biometrics, or launching an information system without an appropriate privacy impact assessment can create Law 25 exposure.
Technology and AI RiskCookies, tracking, session replay, facial or voice analysis, automated transcription, AI moderation, profiling, automated decisions and global platforms can trigger privacy by default, transparency, biometric, automated-decision, security, vendor and impact-assessment requirements.
Consent and Purpose RiskUsing personal information outside the stated research purpose, relying on inadequate consent or notice, or converting research information into marketing or lead generation can create privacy, anti-spam and ethical exposure.
Indigenous Data RiskFailing to engage appropriate First Nations, Inuit or Métis authorities or communities, or overlooking data sovereignty, governance, benefit and cultural protocols, can cause harm and undermine access, validity and ethics.
CASL and Digital Communications RiskEmail, SMS, cookies, SDKs, pixels, session replay, online identifiers and tracking can create separate communications and privacy obligations even when the project is described as research.
Sensitive Data RiskHealth, race or ethnicity, religion, political opinions, union membership, genetic, biometric, sex-life, sexual-orientation, precise location and financial information require heightened legal, ethical and security assessment.
Data Sharing RiskGiving raw respondent data, recordings or identifiers to a client, affiliate, platform or vendor can change consent, purpose, organisation role, notice, security and out-of-Quebec communication analysis.
International Processing RiskGlobal access to Quebec participant data can require privacy impact assessment, contractual safeguards, transparency, security and provincial analysis before access is enabled.
Sampling and Fraud RiskDuplicate, inattentive, automated or misrepresented respondents, panel conditioning and unclear weighting can affect digital samples unless quality controls are proportionate and documented.
Interpretation RiskSmall qualitative samples, opt-in panels, modelled estimates and subgroup results may be generalised beyond what the method supports.
Confidentiality RiskUnreleased products, public-service information, investment theses, client identities, community information or identifiable interviews may be exposed through weak agency, facility, platform or reporting controls.

FAQ

The following questions address common commercial and operational issues for organisations commissioning market research in Quebec.

Is market research a regulated profession in Quebec?No dedicated statutory professional licence generally applies to commercial market research providers. Specific work is shaped by Quebec Law 25, French language law, PIPEDA in specified contexts, CASL, consumer, advertising, sector, contractual and professional-standard requirements.
Does Law 25 apply to Quebec market research?It can apply broadly where a private enterprise collects, holds, uses or communicates personal information in Quebec. The project should assess the organisation, data, purpose, consent, technology, vendors, privacy impact assessment, communications outside Quebec and current CAI requirements.
Does Quebec have a revenue threshold like some US privacy laws?Quebec privacy law is not based on a broad general small-business revenue threshold like several US state laws. The activity and personal information context should be assessed rather than assuming a small organisation is outside scope.
Must Quebec market research be in French?Not every research assignment has identical language duties, but French is Quebec’s official language and French-language materials are often necessary for target coverage, commercial communication, participant experience and applicable language-law compliance. Assess each participant population and commissioning context.
When is a privacy impact assessment required?Law 25 requires a privacy impact assessment before acquiring, developing or overhauling an information system or electronic service delivery system involving personal information, and before communicating personal information outside Quebec. Other research and statistical disclosure contexts can also require assessment.
Can Quebec participant data be hosted outside Quebec or Canada?Potentially, but communication outside Quebec must be assessed through a privacy impact assessment and appropriate safeguards. Recipient roles, country, legal framework, purpose, data categories, security, contract, transparency and retention should be established before access begins.
Can a client receive raw respondent data?Only where delivery is compatible with research purpose, consent or other legal authority, Law 25, privacy impact, participant information, minimisation, confidentiality, security and contract. Aggregated or de-identified delivery is often preferable.
Can Montreal research represent Quebec?Not automatically. Quebec-wide claims should correspond to the sample frame, including regions, language, urban-rural and northern coverage, age, income, Indigenous identity, digital access and other relevant dimensions.
Can research recruitment include a sales offer?Research and commercial solicitation should be separated. Mixed activity can trigger participant trust, Law 25, CASL, consumer, advertising and professional-ethics issues.
Can research findings be used in advertising?Yes, but public claims should be accurate, supportable and not misleading. The research design, population, question wording, analysis and limitations should support the exact message communicated.
